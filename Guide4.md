# Guide 4 — Contact & Final Cinematic Outro

## 0. Purpose

This is the final chapter of the Harry Jees portfolio.

Guide 4 contains:

1. **Contact**
2. **Final Cinematic Outro**

This should not feel like a normal website footer.

It should feel like the natural conclusion of the cinematic journey established throughout Guides 1–3.

The experience should move from:

**Who I am → What I build → What I believe in → What I share → Let's connect**

The ending should be memorable without becoming overly dramatic or cluttered.

---

# 1. Contact Section

## 1.1 Concept

Create a clean, highly visible, minimal contact section.

Do not make it look like a conventional contact form unless a form is already part of the project.

The primary purpose is to make it extremely easy for someone to contact or connect with Harry.

The section should communicate:

**“Let's build something meaningful together.”**

This should be the main closing message.

# 4. Contact Interaction

The interaction should be subtle.

On hover/focus:

* text can shift slightly,
* an underline can animate,
* the active item can become more prominent,
* surrounding items can subtly reduce emphasis if appropriate.

Do not use huge hover transformations.

Do not create unnecessary cursor effects.

The user should immediately understand what each link does.

Accessibility is important:

* keyboard focus must be visible,
* links must have meaningful labels,
* sufficient contrast must be maintained,
* touch targets must be usable on mobile.

---

# 5. Contact Animation

Do not reveal everything instantly.

As the visitor reaches the section:

1. Main heading appears.
2. Closing statement follows.
3. Contact links appear progressively.
4. The section settles.
5. Give the visitor enough time to read and interact.

The animation must be slow and deliberate.

Nothing should feel rushed.

Use GSAP for sequencing if appropriate.

Use Lenis for smooth scrolling.

Do not add unnecessary 3D effects simply because this is the final section.

---

# 6. Final Cinematic Outro

The portfolio should NOT simply end with a normal footer.

After the visitor has reached the Contact section and the content has had enough time to settle, transition into the final cinematic identity moment.

The final scene should remain connected to the visual language of the Hero.

---

# 7. Outro Concept

The ending should feel like the portfolio is closing its visual loop.

The Hero began with:

**particles → identity → Harry Jees**

The ending can reverse that idea:

**Contact → identity → particles → fade**

The final sequence should feel like the experience is gently dissolving rather than suddenly stopping.

---

# 8. Final Particle Identity

Reuse the existing particle technology from the Hero where technically practical.

Do not create an unrelated second particle system merely for decoration.

The existing particle system should be treated as a recurring visual language.

Possible sequence:

1. Contact section remains visible.
2. The environment becomes quieter.
3. Subtle particles begin appearing.
4. The contact content gradually loses visual emphasis.
5. Particles gather into the **Harry Jees** identity.
6. The identity holds briefly.
7. The particle field gently disperses.
8. The scene fades toward black.

The animation must be slow enough to be appreciated.

Do not make the particles suddenly explode or disappear.

---

# 9. Do Not Destroy the Hero Particle System

The Hero's particle transformation is one of the signature features of the portfolio.

The final outro should reuse its visual language without modifying the Hero's established behavior.

If the existing particle architecture can support the outro cleanly, reuse it.

If not, create a lightweight compatible version rather than damaging the original implementation.

The Hero must continue working exactly as intended.

---

# 11. Visual Style

The Contact and Outro must follow the visual rules established throughout the portfolio.

Prioritize:

* pure black
* white
* strong contrast
* clean typography
* generous negative space
* subtle depth
* controlled motion
* cinematic pacing

Avoid:

* gray-heavy UI
* low-contrast text
* unnecessary gradients
* random SVGs
* random images
* decorative illustrations
* excessive glowing effects
* excessive cards
* generic footer layouts
* huge social-media icon grids
* unnecessary 3D objects

Do not add visual elements simply to make the screen look "full."

Empty space is intentional.

---

# 12. Responsive Behavior

The Contact section must work beautifully on:

* Desktop
* Tablet
* Mobile

On mobile:

* stack contact information naturally,
* keep links readable,
* avoid horizontal overflow,
* maintain large enough touch targets,
* simplify the outro if necessary for performance,
* preserve the cinematic feeling.

Do not simply scale the desktop layout down.

---

# 13. Accessibility

Make sure:

* all contact links are keyboard accessible,
* focus states are visible,
* text contrast is strong,
* animations respect reduced-motion preferences where appropriate,
* contact information remains usable even if WebGL is unavailable,
* the page remains understandable without the cinematic effects.

The visual effects should enhance the portfolio, not become a requirement for using it.

---

# 14. Performance

The final particle sequence must not cause unnecessary performance problems.

Reuse existing geometry, materials and systems where possible.

Clean up:

* WebGL resources,
* event listeners,
* animation timelines,
* scroll triggers,
* unused objects.

Avoid leaving multiple particle systems running simultaneously when they are not needed.

---

# 15. Complete Portfolio Flow

After Guide 4, the complete experience should feel like:

**Hero**
→ **About**
→ **Skills**
→ **Projects**
→ **Youth Korp**
→ **Journey**
→ **Speaking / Teaching**
→ **Contact**
→ **Final Cinematic Outro**

The entire website should feel like one continuous interactive story.

---

# 16. Final Quality Requirement

Do not treat this as simply adding a contact section and footer.

This is the conclusion of the entire experience.

The visitor should finish with a clear understanding of:

**Harry Jees — someone who builds things, explores ideas, creates products, works with people, and shares what he learns.**

The final emotional progression should be:

**curiosity → discovery → proof → personality → connection → identity**

Keep it natural.

Keep it minimal.

Keep it highly visible.

Keep the animation slow.

Do not over-design it.

The final moment should leave the visitor thinking:

# **“I want to know what Harry builds next.”**
